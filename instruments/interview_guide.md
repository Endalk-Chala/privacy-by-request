# Semi-structured interview guide (subset, 45 minutes)

Conducted after the session, usually within two weeks, with participants who consented to a follow-up. Audio-recorded with permission; transcribed; pseudonymised; coded per `codebook/interview_codes.md`.

## Opening

1. When we looked at those websites together, what stayed with you afterwards? Did you talk to anyone about it?

## Source attribution (code: source_attribution)

2. Where do you think that information came from? Walk me through how you imagine it got there.
3. (If the participant names a settlement event) Did you know at the time that it could end up anywhere else?

## Imagined searcher (code: imagined_searcher_records)

4. Who do you imagine would look you up on a site like that? Who worries you most?
5. Is that different from who you imagine looking at your Facebook or TikTok? At your résumé? (imagined_audience_platforms, imagined_audience_resume)

## Unit of harm (code: unit_of_harm)

6. When you saw your relatives' names listed, what did that mean to you? Is this something that happens to you, or to your family, or to your community?

## Vocabulary (codes: vocabulary_term, vocabulary_gloss)

7. In your language, what word would you use for what we saw? Is there a word for privacy? What does it cover and what does it not cover?
8. (code: comparison_to_home) How does this compare with how information about people worked where you grew up?

## Response repertoire (code: response_repertoire)

9. Now that you know, what, if anything, will you do? What would you need in order to do it?
10. (If removal services were explained in the debrief) Would you pay for that? What would it cost that is too much?
11. Who would you ask for help with this, if anyone?

## Discovery (code: discovery_event_described)

12. Before our session, had anything ever made you realise your information was out there? Tell me about that moment.

## Closing

13. Is there anything you think I should ask other people that I did not ask you?

## Coding

Each transcript is coded deductively against the eleven codes. A 25% random sample is double-coded; disagreements are resolved by discussion and recorded. Quotations selected for publication are returned to the participant for approval (consent_quotes = 1 only).
